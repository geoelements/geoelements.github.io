## About

Krishna Kumar is an Associate Professor of Civil and Environmental Engineering at UC Berkeley. He joins Berkeley in January 2027 through the College of Engineering's Engineering + AI initiative. Before Berkeley, he was the J. Neils Thompson Centennial Teaching Fellow and an Associate Professor in the Maseeh Department of Civil, Architectural and Environmental Engineering at the University of Texas at Austin, and core faculty at the Oden Institute for Computational Engineering and Sciences.

Krishna develops computational methods that help robots predict and act in unfamiliar physical environments. His research combines computational mechanics, machine learning, and robotics, with a focus on deformable materials and complex physical interactions. His group infers material behavior from limited observations and uses the identified models for fast prediction, planning, and control. The group develops weak-form methods that identify constitutive behavior from observed motion, graph-based learned simulators for particle dynamics, and operator-learning methods for efficient simulation. Applications include construction automation, soft-material manipulation, navigation on deformable terrain, lunar excavation, and natural hazards.

Krishna directs [Chishiki.ai](https://www.chishiki-ai.org/), a $7M NSF-funded national community for AI in civil and environmental engineering. He has contributed to NSF's DesignSafe-CI cyberinfrastructure since 2019. He is a co-founder of Tektos AI, which works on construction automation and data centers. He received his Ph.D. from the University of Cambridge in 2015 under the supervision of Professor Kenichi Soga, and he was a Research Associate and Bye-Fellow at King's College, Cambridge.
